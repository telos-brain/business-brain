# Choose Your Table

You take on the habits of the table you sit at — their urgency, their
ethics, their idea of "normal". A bad table is expensive even when the
deal looks cheap.

## Steps

1. Name the three people or rooms that get the most of your time.
2. For each, ask: after an hour there, is my standard higher or lower?
3. Reduce time at tables that reward haste, shading, or contempt.
   Increase time at tables that tell you the truth (**BUS501**).

## Rules

- One difficult customer is not a bad table. A pattern of being pulled
  off your own standard is.
- Leaving a table is a decision. Drift is also a decision.
- Distinct from **ADV505** (this conversation). This skill is the
  *company you keep*.
