# Company blueprint

Start by reading the blueprint. The prompt lists every blueprint at this tier, with its description and categories. Find **Company** and read that description and the category you are writing before you apply the rules below.

This memory is for a later reader. When someone is mentioned, they load the entry and learn who the person is, what the company is, or what a system is for. They also see the specific names of other entries worth loading next. There is no link field. Connection is a precise name. If you cannot name the other entry exactly, do not imply a connection.

Every sentence must be supported by the task and the entry body. If the source does not establish the fact, do not write it. A thin source is a skipped write.

## Team

One entry per person who works in this business. The title is their name.

A transcript names many people. Most of them are not team. Write a Team entry only when the source establishes that this person works here. A client, a partner, a supplier, a candidate, someone quoted, or someone who sat in the meeting from outside is not Team. If you cannot tell, do not write.

Search that name before you create. Update the existing entry when it is the same person. Do not open a second entry because this meeting added a detail, and do not split one person across a first name and a full name. If you cannot tell whether two names are the same person, do not create another entry.

Record what will still explain this person later:

- Role
- Responsibilities — the standing work they hold, not the task they are on this week
- Key relationships — name the other person, company, system, or offering exactly, so that entry can be loaded beside this one

Do not record what they said, a commitment that expires this week, leave, sick days, personal life, or small talk. Do not record how to do the job. That is a skill.

## Vision and values

What this company is: its vision, what it does, and the values that govern how it operates. This is a standing description of the company, updated in place. Keep few entries.

This category is easy to invent and easy to contaminate. Write a sentence only when the source states it as this company's vision, as what this company does, or as a value it holds. Do not infer values from behaviour, tone, or a single decision. Do not borrow a client's vision or values, even when the meeting was about them. Do not file current priorities here — that is Strategy. Do not file a procedure — that is a skill.

"How it operates" means the standing way the company says it conducts itself. It does not mean this week's plan, and it does not mean the steps of a process.

## Strategy

Durable direction for this business: how it wins, the priorities that set direction, and decisions that set direction. Company strategy only. Keep few entries and update them when direction changes.

A strategy being discussed for a client does not belong here. Vision, what the company does, and its values do not belong here — those are Vision and values. A plan for the week, a planning meeting, or a list of tasks for the year is not strategy. The method for making strategy is a skill.

Do not invent direction. If the source did not state a priority or a choice for this business, do not write one.

## Systems

One entry per system the business actually uses. The title is the name people use for it.

Mentioning a system, comparing one, or considering one is not use. Write the entry only when the source shows this business runs work through it. That includes software and systems that are not software: a spreadsheet, a shared file, or another offline routine. The title can be the purpose of that routine when the source describes a real routine and gives it no product name.

Each entry has two sections, and only these two:

```markdown
## The system

What it is.

## Who uses it and why

Who uses it, how it is deployed, and the purpose it serves.
```

Write only the parts the source supports. Leave a section short rather than fill it. Do not record the steps for operating it. That is a skill. Do not record a tool this business sells — that is Products and services.

## Products and services

One entry per product or service this business sells. The title is the offering.

Be specific about what is sold: what it is, who it is for, and how it is positioned. A vague label is not an offering unless the source says that is the thing sold and what it includes. Do not record the delivery process. Do not record one customer's purchase — that belongs on CRM or the job.

Search the offering name before you create. Update the same entry when the description changes. Do not open a second entry for a new detail about the same offering.
