# Business blueprint

Start by reading the blueprint. The prompt lists every blueprint at this tier, with its description and categories. Find **Business** and read that description and the category you are writing before you apply the rules below.

This memory is for a later reader. They load an entry and learn who a person is, what a customer is, what an area covers, what the direction is, or what was decided. They also see the exact titles of other entries worth loading next. There is no link field. Connection is a precise title. If you cannot name the other entry exactly, do not imply a connection.

Every sentence must be supported by the task and the entry body. If the source does not establish the fact, do not write it. A thin source is a skipped write.

## How an entry is shaped

People, Areas, Strategy, and Customers open with a summary, then the standing detail, then dated facts when a fact is tied to a time:

```markdown
## Summary

Who or what this is, and why it matters. Updated in place.

Standing detail. Correct it in place when the new sentence simply replaces the old one.

## 9 Nov 2026

A fact about that day, or a change whose earlier wording should stay. Latest date first.
```

The date format is `d MMM yyyy`: day with no leading zero, a three-letter English month, and a four-digit year. `5 Sep 2026` and `9 Nov 2026` are right. Any other date format is wrong.

Use a date heading when the fact is about a particular day, or you are updating an entry and the previous text is still worth keeping because the fact moves. Leave the older dated section in place. One heading per date. If that date already has a heading, add the new fact under it. The heading is the date the fact is about, when the source gives one. Otherwise use the inbox entry date. Do not invent a date. The title stays the name of the thing. The date lives in the body.

Do not date a standing sentence that should simply be corrected: who a person is, a role, what an area is, a direction that has been replaced. Replace that text in place.

A Journal entry opens with the decision or the learning, includes its date in the same format, and stops when a later reader could use it. It does not need a Summary heading.

## People

One entry per person this business has a relationship with. Staff, a customer's people, a partner, a supplier, anyone we deal with. The title is their name.

A name in a transcript is not enough. Write only when the source shows a relationship worth keeping. If you cannot tell who they are, do not write.

Search that name before you create. Update the existing entry when it is the same person. Do not open a second entry because this meeting added a detail, and do not split one person across a first name and a full name. If you cannot tell whether two names are the same person, do not create another entry. A first name alone does not clear the bar unless the entry also names their organisation, exactly, so that entry can be loaded beside it.

The summary says who they are and why they matter here. Under it, record what will still explain them later:

- Role
- Organisation — the customer or other entry, named exactly
- What matters in the relationship

Date a fact that is tied to a time, or that will change and should keep its earlier wording.

Do not record what they said in passing, a commitment that expires this week, leave, personal life, or small talk. Do not record how to do the job. That is a skill. Do not record the customer organisation on this entry beyond the name — the standing of the customer is Customers.

## Areas

One entry per area being tracked. The title is the name people would use to find it again: Finance, a named project, a kind of supplier, a named problem, a system the business runs, an offering, a campaign.

Search that name before you create. Update the same entry. Do not open a second entry for a new detail about the same area.

The summary says what the area is and why it is being tracked. Under it, record the standing a later reader needs. Date what is tied to a time.

Mentioning a system, or discussing a project that was not taken up, is not an area. Write it when the source shows the business is actually tracking it.

Do not record the steps for operating it. That is a skill. Do not record one customer — that is Customers. Do not record purpose, mission, or long-term position — that is Strategy. A decision made while working an area, if a later reader would reuse it, is Journal. Name the area from that journal entry.

## Journal

One entry per decision or learning that a future reader would use.

Write it when one of these is true:

- A decision was made, and the next time should follow it or should not repeat it
- Something was learned that would make the next attempt better
- Something useful had to be searched out, and the value is remembering where it lives

The title names the decision or the learning. Open with the point. Include the date. Name a person, customer, area, or strategy entry by its exact title when the learning points at it. If the procedure lives in a skill, name that skill's code.

Do not write it when it is only a record that work happened. The system already keeps that record. Do not transcribe a meeting, a task list, or a sequence of actions. Options that were only discussed are not a decision. A repeatable method is a skill, not a journal entry. Long-term direction is Strategy. The area being tracked stays on Areas; the journal entry points at it.

If you cannot say what a later reader would do differently, or what they would now know where to find, do not write.

## Strategy

Purpose, mission, long-term positioning, the target customer, and the path to market. Company direction only. Keep few entries and update them when the direction changes.

The summary states the direction. Under it, record what should still hold over a long horizon. Do not invent it. If the source did not state a purpose, a position, or a long-term choice for this business, do not write one.

A campaign, a project, or a problem in front of the business is an Area. A tactical choice, or a lesson from doing the work, is Journal. The method for setting direction is a skill.

## Customers

One entry per customer. The title is the customer name — specific enough to find that customer again. "The client" is not an entry.

Search that name before you create. Update the same entry when the standing changes.

The summary says who they are to this business. Under it, record what we do for them, what is already agreed, and the constraints that will still matter. Date what is tied to a time.

When you name a person at that customer, use the same title as their People entry. On the person, name this customer exactly. Do not copy the person's detail onto the customer, and do not copy the customer's standing onto the person.

A project for this customer is an Area. Name the customer from that area. Do not record how selling or delivery is done. Do not record this business's purpose or path to market.

## The bar

Do not create an entry you could not find again. If you cannot tell which person, customer, area, or decision the source means, do not write.
