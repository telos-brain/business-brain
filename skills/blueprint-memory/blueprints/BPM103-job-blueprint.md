# Job blueprint

Start by reading the blueprint. The prompt lists every blueprint at this tier, with its description and categories. Find **Job** and read that description and the category you are writing before you apply the rules below.

This blueprint is the work itself: what the job is, the decisions made for it, and where it stands. It is not the company's method for doing this kind of work, and it is not the standing file for a company or a person.

Entries connect by specific names. When the job is for a company, or a person is part of it, use the same name as that CRM entry so a later reader can load both. There is no link field. A vague "the client" or a first name with no company does not make a connection. If you cannot name them specifically, leave them out of the job entry rather than store a name that cannot be followed.

Every sentence must be supported by the task and the entry body. Do not invent an outcome, a decision, an owner, or a blocker.

## Brief

One entry per piece of work. The title is the job. Search that name before you create, and update the same entry in place.

Record the outcome, the constraints that define the work, and who it is for — the company or person, named specifically when the source gives that name. Do not record the general method for scoping work. Do not paste a transcript. Do not record choices already made (Decisions) or where it stands now (State).

## Decisions

One entry per decision made for a piece of work that should not be relitigated. The title names the job and the decision.

Record what was chosen, what was rejected, and why — only where the source says the choice was made. Options that were only discussed are not a decision. A general decision-making method is a skill. Do not open a second entry for the same decision because the meeting restated it.

## State

One entry per piece of work. The title is the job — the same name as its Brief. Search that name and update in place.

Record the owner, the status, and what is blocking it, when the source establishes each of those. Name the owner specifically when they are a person this brain already distinguishes (a team member, or a person on CRM). Do not log every meeting, a weekly task list, or small talk. Do not record the process for running this kind of work.

Status moves. When you update State and the previous standing is still worth keeping, follow the workflow's date-heading rules. Do not put the date in the title.
