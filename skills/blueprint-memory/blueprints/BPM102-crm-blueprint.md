# CRM blueprint

Start by reading the blueprint. The prompt lists every blueprint at this tier, with its description and categories. Find **CRM** and read that description and the category you are writing before you apply the rules below.

This blueprint is the record of the companies and people this business deals with: clients, partners, industry contacts, and prospects. It is not our own team, not our strategy, and not a job's brief or status.

A later reader follows a name from one entry to another by loading both. There is no link field. The name has to be specific enough to find. If the information is vague, it is not worth storing.

Every sentence must be supported by the task and the entry body. Do not invent a company, a role, a term, or a relationship the source did not establish.

## The bar

Do not create an entry you could not find again.

- A company needs a name that identifies that organisation, not "the client", "a partner", or a description with no name.
- A person needs a name that identifies them. A first name alone does not clear the bar unless the entry also names the company they sit in, exactly, so the company entry can be loaded beside it.
- If you have a person and cannot name their company, and the name is not already specific, do not write the person.
- If you cannot tell which company or which person the source means, do not write.

## Companies

One entry per organisation. The title is the company name. Search that name before you create. Update the same entry when the standing changes. Do not open a second entry because this meeting added a detail.

Record what it does, and the kind of tie: client, partner, industry, or prospect. Record what is already agreed — terms, pricing, or the shape of a partnership — and the commitments or constraints that will still matter after this week. A partner, an industry body, or a prospect may have no commercial terms. Record the standing anyway.

When you name a person at that company, use the same name as their People entry, so the two can be loaded together.

Do not record the people in full on this entry — that is People. Do not record our own company. Do not record how selling, partnering, or invoicing is done. Do not record a job's brief, decisions, or current status. Do not record this week's task list.

## People

One entry per person this business deals with. The title is the person's name. Search that name, and the company name, before you create. Update the same person. Do not create a second entry for a shorter form of a name you already hold.

Record their role, which organisation they sit in (the company name, exactly), what they care about in the relationship, and how they are involved. The kind of tie — client, partner, industry, or prospect — stays on the company. Do not copy it onto the person. When the person has no organisation, record the kind of tie here, and only if the person's name is specific enough to find again.

Do not record our own team — that is Company Team. Do not record personal life, small talk, or what they happen to be doing this week. Do not record how we manage relationships.
