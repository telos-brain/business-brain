# Second-order Effects

The first-order effect is what the plan is *for*. The second-order
effect is what other people, systems or incentives do in response.
Cheap advice stops at the first.

## Steps

1. State the intended first-order effect in one sentence.
2. Ask **and then what?** Who adjusts — a customer, a competitor, a
   team, a regulator, a metric-gamer?
3. Ask **and then what?** again on that response. Stop at two hops
   unless a third is obvious and material.
4. Keep only effects that would change the advice. Mention them as
   gaps or conditions, not a systems essay.

## Rules

- Two hops. A third is rare. A fourth is theatre.
- Do not stack speculative disasters. Each hop needs a plausible actor
  with a reason to move.
- A second-order effect that you can watch and reverse is a reason to
  go ahead carefully (**DEC701**), not a reason to freeze.
