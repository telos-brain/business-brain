# Pre-mortem the Plan

Gary Klein's pre-mortem. The move that matters is the **crystal ball**:
the plan has *already* failed. Not "what might go wrong?" — that question
is too gentle and produces polite risks.

This skill reviews a **plan**. For a diagnosis or root cause, use
**DEC101** instead.

## Steps

1. State the plan in two sentences (goal, approach, time horizon).
2. Set the frame: it is later, and this has failed badly. That is a fact
   for the next two minutes.
3. Write **three to five** specific reasons why. Prefer the things people
   usually will not say (politics, fading sponsor, the constraint nobody
   checked).
4. Keep the **two** that are most damaging or most likely. For each, name
   one preventive move you can make *now*.

## Rules

- Specificity beats categories. "Stakeholders unhappy" is useless.
  "Finance withdraws the budget when the first milestone slips" is a
  reason.
- Do not run a long risk register. Two preventable failure modes are
  enough for an advice call.
- If you cannot imagine a real failure, the plan may be too vague to
  challenge. Bound it (**ADV201**) and try again.
