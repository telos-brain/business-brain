# Bound the Ask

The first sentence is often a symptom, a preferred solution, or a mood — not
the decision. Bound the ask before you advise.

## Steps

1. Write three lines:
   - **Presenting ask** — what they said they want.
   - **Situation** — what appears to be going on (facts only).
   - **Open decision** — the choice that is actually still available.
2. Ask whether the presenting ask pre-loads a solution ("how do we ship X")
   when the open decision is whether X is the right move.
3. If the open decision is different from the ask, name it and advise on
   *that*. Say that you have reframed.

## Rules

- Do not invent a deeper problem to look clever. If the ask *is* the
  decision, keep it.
- A how-to question with an untested goal is usually unbounded. A yes/no
  on a named option is usually bounded.
- If two decisions are tangled (diagnose *and* choose a fix), split them.
  Diagnose first.
