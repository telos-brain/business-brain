# Steelmanning

The opposite of a straw man. Rebuild the case until its author would say
"yes, that is what I meant" — then advise against *that* version.

A steelman is **accurate, charitable, and complete**: it keeps their
claim, resolves ambiguity in the direction that makes the case stronger,
and includes the reason, not just the headline.

## Steps

1. Restate the thinking in two or three sentences, stronger and cleaner
   than the original wording if needed — but still *their* case.
2. Check: would a fair reader recognise this as the same argument? If
   not, you have invented a different one.
3. Only then name the gap, the missing constraint, or the better option.

## Rules

- Do not attack a sloppy sentence when a tighter reading still stands.
- Do not **mithrilman**: do not invent a brilliant case they did not make
  and then praise or demolish that. Charity is not deference.
- If the thinking is already sound, the steelman *is* the advice. Say so
  and stop. Do not manufacture a gap.
